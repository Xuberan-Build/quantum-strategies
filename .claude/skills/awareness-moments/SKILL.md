# Awareness and micro-moments

Austin, 2026-11-01: "like you need to answer like what's in it for me subtily in these articles. Yes the science needs to be good but also be really considering those micro moments for our audience"

Full reference (sourced audience words per stage, every move with examples and pitfalls, sources): `brain/content/awareness-and-micro-moments.md`. Read it once per session.

## When to use it

Brief: plan it (section 13). Draft or revision: write it. QA: run the rubric as check 12. Published pieces: run the rubric, fix only what fails. Use it with `qs-voice` and the gates in `brain/content/pipeline/README.md` §3 and §7. Evidence and safety always win over this layer.

## The principle

Every section answers the reader's "so what does this mean for me?" without selling. After each finding, write one sentence that moves it into the reader's ordinary day: their week, their work, the thing they keep meaning to get to. Use their words from `brain/research/conversation/` and `brain/research/language/audience-vocabulary.md`.

- Keep the bridge at the finding's tier. A lab finding is "consistent with", never "proven in your life".
- Say what they may notice or try, never what they will get. Banned: "you will", "transform", speed, income or health promises.
- The benefit is recognition, a better reading of what they already live, and one free step this week.
- Cite a finding in reader copy only if the brief's allowed-evidence table lists it as checked. Several mechanism sources are still VERIFY in `brain/strategy/thesis-evidence-map.md`.

## The moves (checklist)

- [ ] **M1 Answer first.** Two sentences: the direct answer at the strongest source's tier, then what it means for their week.
- [ ] **M2 Stage.** Label the reader in the brief. Problem-aware opens on the felt problem, solution-aware on why fixes stalled. Name QS only near the end.
- [ ] **M3 Recognition.** One specific, observable everyday pattern with a time, a place and its cost that day. Falsifiable: a reader who doesn't do it can say "not me". No Barnum lines, no clinical labels, no chart causation.
- [ ] **M4 Bridge.** One so-what sentence after every finding. Never two in a row.
- [ ] **M5 Open loop.** A real tension stated early in a section and closed inside it, within 3 or 4 paragraphs. One at a time. Never closed only behind the CTA.
- [ ] **M6 Self-test.** One question answerable from their own week in 30 seconds, before the mechanism. No score, no diagnosis.
- [ ] **M7 Mechanism.** Plain words, no metaphors, study type inline, then a bridge. Practitioner models labelled as Austin's practice.
- [ ] **M8 Reframe (the aha).** The common reading, then the better-supported reading with its evidence, as two flat sentences. No quote-graphic lines, no "it's not X, it's Y".
- [ ] **M9 Objection.** State the strongest audience doubt fairly, grant its true part, answer it with evidence or a limit.
- [ ] **M10 Limits.** One paragraph: study type, and one thing the evidence doesn't show.
- [ ] **M11 Austin slot.** Lived moments are `[AUSTIN: what is needed, one line]` unless anchored to `input.md` or `experience.md`.
- [ ] **M12 Do-step.** One free action under 10 minutes, with a done-state and what to notice. Works without the product. Never "read or watch more".
- [ ] **M13 Subheads.** Each H2 is their question or the consequence in their words; the first sentence answers it.
- [ ] **M14 Close.** One or two sentences on the next step for their stage, the standard CTA, LIVE vs TO BUILD plain, ending on the next step or what done looks like.

## Plan the moments in a brief

Write brief section 13, **Awareness and micro-moments**:

1. Stage label, and the evidence for it (query wording, quotes, which sample).
2. Micro-moment (know or do) and the two-sentence answer the opening gives.
3. Problem frame (reference §3) and 3 to 5 verbatim audience phrases with locators.
4. The aha: common reading vs better-supported reading, with claim id, tier and status.
5. Bridge plan: one row per finding (finding, tier, checked or VERIFY, the everyday moment it lands in).
6. The objection to pre-empt and what answers it.
7. The do-step, its done-state and what to notice.
8. Next step for the stage, with LIVE, BUILT or TO BUILD, matching sections 6 and 8.
9. Austin slots this layer needs, handed to content-experience as questions.

## Place them in a draft

Order (reference §5): H1 as their question, answer and first bridge, recognition, open loop, self-test, mechanism and bridge (loop closes), reframe, Austin's moment (move it up if the first-hand gate needs it in the first third), objection, limits, do-step, FAQ (3 to 5 real audience questions), scoped close and CTA. Scripts: the hook is the recognition moment, one of the 3 moves is the do-step, the caveat is the limits line.

Then read only the H2s and first sentences. If a skimmer can't tell what's in it for them, rewrite the subheads.

## QA rubric (content-qa check 12)

Mark each PASS or FAIL and quote the line. **Blocking:** AM3, AM4, AM9, AM11, AM12.

| # | Fails when |
|---|---|
| AM1 Stage | No stage label in the brief, or the opening assumes another stage |
| AM2 Answer first | The first two sentences don't answer the search, or open on a rhetorical question, "Imagine", a definition or a scene |
| AM3 Recognition | No specific, falsifiable everyday pattern; a Barnum line; a clinical label about the reader; a chart as cause |
| AM4 Bridges | A cited finding with no bridge; two bridges in a row; a bridge above the tier; any promise of outcome, speed, income or health |
| AM5 Loops | A loop not closed in its own section, or closed only behind the CTA |
| AM6 Aha | No two-reading reframe, or it's a slogan, a contrast frame, or blames the reader |
| AM7 Objection | The strongest doubt is missing, left unanswered, or answered with a canon line |
| AM8 Limits | No limits paragraph, or a VERIFY source cited publicly |
| AM9 Do-step | No free step under 10 minutes with a done-state, it needs the product, or it recommends more content |
| AM10 Subheads | The H2s alone don't carry the WIIFM |
| AM11 Next step | Beta described beyond ArticleCta; the Read pushed while off; a link to `/products/perception`; the want-or-should step promised; "founding member" or an internal framework named |
| AM12 Austin | Any first-person moment written for him without an anchor |

## Hard rules

Never invent Austin's material. No em dashes. Never "founding member" (Initiation, Activation, Operator only). No internal framework names in reader copy. No health claims, no promised outcomes, charts are raw data never the cause (`brain/content/safety-screen.md`). Nobody caused their problems by thinking the wrong thoughts. Claims stay at tier (`brain/content/pipeline/runs/ras-cluster-source-check.md`, `brain/strategy/thesis-evidence-map.md`).
